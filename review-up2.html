UP 2 MË

Up 2 Më: Yeat’s Glitchy Ascent to Rage Rap Stardom


On September 10, 2021, Portland rapper Yeat dropped Up 2 Më, a 22-track debut album that stormed the underground with viral fury. Following a string of buzzworthy singles, this project thrust Yeat into the spotlight as a torchbearer of the rage rap wave. As of March 16, 2025, let’s dive into this chaotic, adrenaline-pumping triumph.


The Messaging and Theme

The album’s cover—a minimalist red and black swirl—hints at its vibe: a whirlwind of excess and emotion. Up 2 Më captures Yeat reveling in his ascent, flexing wealth in “Monëy So Big” and taunting foes in “Gët Busy.” Beneath the bravado, tracks like “Lët Ya Know” reveal flickers of vulnerability—“This could be my last song, ho”—amid tales of drugs and despair. It’s not deep poetry; it’s a raw snapshot of a 21-year-old riding fame’s high, fueled by lean and Tonkas. This is rage rap distilled—unapologetic, reckless, and defiantly youthful.


Production and Sound

With over 20 producers like BNYX, F1LTHY, and Synthetic, Up 2 Më delivers a sonic explosion of rage beats—distorted 808s, glitchy synths, and omnipresent bells. “Turban” screeches with warped energy, while “Swërved It” pulses with menacing minimalism. “Monëy So Big” rides a hypnotic horn loop that went TikTok-viral, and “Ya Ya” thumps with bass-heavy euphoria. Yeat’s voice—elastic, Auto-Tuned, and layered with ad-libs—morphs into an instrument, bending around the beats. It’s a fresh twist on Whole Lotta Red’s blueprint, though the sheer volume of tracks risks monotony.


Features

Up 2 Më is a solo showcase with one exception: SeptembersRich on “Trëndy Way.” His laid-back flow complements Yeat’s intensity, adding a mellow counterpoint to the track’s twitchy beat. The scarcity of guests keeps the focus on Yeat, a bold move that pays off—his quirky charisma carries the album. This isn’t a collab-heavy affair; it’s Yeat’s world, and he owns every corner of it.


Tracks to Watch

  • “Gët Busy” - Piercing bells ignite this unhinged banger, Yeat’s viral war cry echoing with relentless energy.

  • “Monëy So Big” - Hypnotic horns anchor this TikTok juggernaut, Yeat’s swagger turning excess into an infectious flex.

  • “Turban” - Warped synths fuel this psychedelic fever dream, Yeat’s elastic flow twisting through glitchy chaos.

  • “Swërved It” - Minimalist beats drip with menace, Yeat’s eerie delivery cementing his rage rap dominance.

Previous Works

Yeat’s journey kicked off with EPs like Deep Blue Strips (2018) and mixtapes like Alivë (2021), where he honed his glitchy, melodic trap style. Singles like “Sorry Bout That” and “Mad Bout That” built underground buzz, but Up 2 Më marks his leap forward. Compared to the looser, experimental 4L earlier in 2021, this album sharpens his sound—more cohesive, more explosive, and primed for mainstream ears. It’s Yeat graduating from SoundCloud obscurity to a rage rap frontrunner.


Summary

Up 2 Më delivers a 56-minute adrenaline rush—22 tracks of unrelenting energy and psychedelic trap vibes. Yeat peaks here, riding the rage wave with infectious hooks and mind-bending production. The album’s length and repetitive beats can wear thin, but its highs—like “Gët Busy”—are undeniable. As of March 16, 2025, Up 2 Më stands as a vibe-first triumph, catapulting Yeat from Portland’s shadows to rap’s forefront—a movement taking root, raw and unpolished yet impossibly catchy.


Ratings

Originality (17/20): Glitchy rage rap evolves with Yeat’s quirky flair, though Carti’s influence lingers in the background.

Production Quality (18/20): Distorted beats dazzle with chaotic energy, a sonic assault tempered by slight repetition.

Lyrics and Messaging (15/20): Playful bars ooze charisma and quotability, prioritizing vibe over depth in true rage fashion.

Enjoyment Factor (19/20): Chaotic energy hooks instantly, an addictive rush begging to be blasted at full volume.

Consistency (16/20): Towering highs dominate, but the 22-track sprawl risks fatigue in its weaker moments.

Overall Rating (85/100): 8.5/10

Up 2 Më isn’t a flawless debut—its length and one-note tendencies show growing pains—but it’s a bold statement from Yeat, a kid who turned TikTok snippets into a cultural moment. Chaotic, mindless fun elevated by stellar beats and a voice that’s impossible to ignore, it stands tall in the crowded rage rap scene—a neon-lit Tonka truck barreling through the underground, proving Yeat’s here to stay.
