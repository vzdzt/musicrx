FLOWER BOY

Flower Boy: Tyler, The Creator’s Lush Awakening Unleashed


On July 21, 2017, Tyler, The Creator dropped Flower Boy, a 14-track, 47-minute bloom via Columbia Records, all self-produced with lush assists from Rex Orange County and others. It hit No. 2 on the Billboard 200 with 106,000 first-week units, snagged a Grammy nod for Best Rap Album, and went Gold by 2018. As of March 11, 2025, its petals still shine—let’s dig into Tyler’s radiant shift.


The Messaging and Theme


The cover—Tyler amidst sunflowers, painted by Eric White—mirrors Flower Boy’s core: a vivid unraveling of self. Shedding Odd Future’s chaos, Tyler bares love, queerness, and isolation. “See You Again” pines—“I wonder if you look both ways”—“Garden Shed” whispers identity’s weight, while “Where This Flower Blooms” celebrates roots—“From a boy to a man.” “Boredom” sighs at solitude. It’s Tyler’s garden—lush, honest, and alive.


Production and Sound


Tyler, The Creator crafts Flower Boy’s soundscape solo, with flourishes from Rex Orange County and crew—jazz, neo-soul, and hip-hop swirling in a pastel haze. “Foreword” growls with strings, “911 / Mr. Lonely” struts on funky bass, “Who Dat Boy” snarls with grit, and “November” hums with warm keys. It’s a leap from Cherry Bomb’s edge—polished, vibrant, and cinematic.


Features


Flower Boy blossoms with guests: Frank Ocean on “Where This Flower Blooms” and “911 / Mr. Lonely,” Kali Uchis on “See You Again,” A$AP Rocky on “Who Dat Boy,” Rex Orange County on “Boredom,” plus Lil Wayne, Estelle, and Anna of the North. They’re petals around Tyler’s stem—vital yet never overshadowing.


Tracks to Watch

  • “See You Again” (feat. Kali Uchis) - Chords drip like honey over a sunlit meadow, Tyler’s wistful croon—“I wonder if you look both ways”—weaving a tender ache through pastel haze, Kali’s sweet hum lifting it into a daydream, a love song that floats soft and fragile, blooming with unrequited longing.

  • “Who Dat Boy” (feat. A$AP Rocky) - Synths growl like a revving engine, a feral jolt of dark adrenaline as Tyler and Rocky trade snarls—“Who dat boy? Who him is?”—over a beat that shifts gears with menace, a chaotic burst of swagger that cuts through the album’s warmth like a jagged thorn.

  • “Garden Shed” (feat. Estelle) - Strings sigh over a hushed glow, Tyler’s confession—“Truth is, since a youth, I’ve been hiding”—unfurling slow and heavy, Estelle’s echo a gentle breeze through the stillness, a raw bloom of identity that roots the album in vulnerability, quiet yet seismic.

  • “911 / Mr. Lonely” (feat. Frank Ocean) - Bass bounces with funky strut, melting into a lonely shimmer as Tyler raps—“My thirst levels are infinity”—Frank’s smooth drift tying the dual vibe, a groove that sways from bold to broken, a kaleidoscope of swagger and solitude in one radiant package.

Previous Works


Tyler’s path to Flower Boy kicked off with Bastard (2009), then Goblin (2011), Wolf (2013), and Cherry Bomb (2015)—raw, chaotic Odd Future roots. Flower Boy refines the noise into melody, paving the way for Igor (2019).


Summary


Flower Boy is a 47-minute tapestry of Tyler’s growth—lush beats and raw soul peaking at No. 2 with 106,000 units, Gold by 2018, and a Metacritic 84 as of March 11, 2025. It’s cohesive and captivating, though “Pothole” dips slightly. A must for vibe chasers.


Ratings

Originality (19/20): Tyler reimagines hip-hop with a floral twist—“Garden Shed” bends norms into soulful confession—a genre-defying leap that fuses jazz, neo-soul, and rap into a radiant new shade, rooted in his Odd Future grit yet soaring beyond, a near-perfect bloom of identity and vision.


Production Quality (20/20): Beats blossom with pristine lushness—“911 / Mr. Lonely” struts and shimmers—every chord, string, and drum sculpted into a vivid, cinematic whole, Tyler’s hands crafting a flawless garden of sound, a masterclass in polish and texture that stands tall without a single wilted note.


Lyrics and Messaging (19/20): Words weave a soulful thread—“See You Again” aches with tender truth—Tyler’s heart laid bare in a tapestry of love, loneliness, and growth, vivid and deep, only softened by brief lighter patches like “Pothole,” a near-masterpiece of introspection that petals out wide.


Enjoyment Factor (19/20): Vibes invite endless spins—“Who Dat Boy” bangs, “November” glows—a rich ride through Tyler’s world, its warmth and depth pulling you in deep, a universal bloom that hooks hard, only slightly less instant for those craving pure chaos over melody.


Consistency (19/20): Tracks unfurl like petals in sync—“Foreword” to “Glitter” flows seamless—each a vibrant piece of a cohesive bouquet, with “Pothole” a subtle dip, a near-flawless arc of beauty and bite that ties Tyler’s awakening into a radiant, unified glow.


Overall Rating (96/100): 9.6/10

Flower Boy is Tyler’s lush triumph—a vibrant pivot from prickly roots to blooming genius, petals of chaos and calm in perfect harmony. Play it loud, let the colors wash, and bask in a near-perfect glow of growth and sound.
