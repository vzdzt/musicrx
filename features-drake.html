DRAKE

The Evolution of a Global Icon: Drake’s Ascendancy


From his early acting role as Jimmy Brooks on Degrassi: The Next Generation to becoming one of the most streamed artists in history with over 170 million records sold worldwide, Aubrey Drake Graham has redefined hip-hop for the streaming era. Born on October 24, 1986, in Toronto, Canada, this multifaceted artist blends rap, R&B, and cultural entrepreneurship, cementing his status as a global icon as of March 15, 2025.


Rise to Fame


Drake’s journey ignited with the 2009 mixtape So Far Gone, a groundbreaking fusion of hip-hop and R&B that introduced hits like "Best I Ever Had" and "Successful". This caught the eye of Lil Wayne, leading to a pivotal signing with Young Money Entertainment. His debut album, Thank Me Later (2010), debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, marking the start of a career defined by innovation and commercial dominance.


Career Milestones

  • 2009: Released So Far Gone, his breakout mixtape, with "Best I Ever Had" hitting No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100, earning a Juno Award for Rap Recording of the Year.

  • 2011: Dropped Take Care, a critical and commercial triumph, winning the Grammy for Best Rap Album and featuring the chart-topping "Take Care" with Rihanna.

  • 2015: Unleashed the surprise mixtape If You’re Reading This It’s Too Late, breaking streaming records with over 500 million streams in its first week, and launched OVO Sound Radio on Beats 1.

  • 2016: Released Views, his highest-selling album to date, spending 13 weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, with "One Dance" becoming his first solo No. 1 hit globally.

  • 2018: Dropped Scorpion, breaking streaming records with 732 million streams in its first week, featuring "God’s Plan" and "In My Feelings", both hitting No. 1.

  • 2021: Released Certified Lover Boy, setting a record with 9 top-10 hits on the Billboard Hot 100 in one week, and expanded OVO with new fashion lines.

  • 2022: Collaborated with 21 Savage on Her Loss, debuting at No. 1 with 404,000 units, and headlined the Aubrey & the Three Migos Tour.

  • 2023: Surpassed 75 billion streams across platforms, released For All the Dogs (debuting at No. 1 with 402,000 units), and was named Billboard’s Artist of the 2010s Decade.

  • 2025: Released Some Sexy Songs 4 U with PartyNextDoor on February 14, debuting at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, and announced a historic three-day headline at the 2025 Wireless Festival, selling out in minutes.

Musical Style & Impact


Drake’s musical alchemy lies in his seamless fusion of rap, R&B, pop, and dancehall, creating a sound that’s uniquely his yet widely emulated. His pioneering use of emotional vulnerability—evident in tracks like "Marvin’s Room" and "Jungle"—paved the way for a new wave of introspective hip-hop artists, including The Weeknd and Bryson Tiller. His production, often crafted with longtime collaborator Noah “40” Shebib, introduces a moody, atmospheric Toronto sound that blends soulful samples with cutting-edge beats. Beyond music, Drake’s OVO Sound label has launched talents like PartyNextDoor and Majid Jordan, while his OVO brand extends into fashion, with Yeezy-like collaborations, and media, including OVO Sound Radio. His influence on language—coining phrases like "6ix" and "YOLO"—and dance trends, like the "In My Feelings" challenge, has reshaped pop culture, making him a cultural entrepreneur par excellence.


Essential Listening

For Newcomers: "Hotline Bling", "God’s Plan", "Started From The Bottom", "One Dance"—these tracks pulse with Drake’s infectious hooks and chart-dominating energy, offering a perfect entry into his melodic mastery and global appeal.

For The Dive: "Marvin’s Room", "Jungle", "Pound Cake" (feat. Jay-Z), "Do Not Disturb", "Take Care" (feat. Rihanna)—dive deep into his emotional landscapes, where haunting melodies and introspective lyrics reveal the soul behind the superstar, backed by lush production.

Hidden Gems: "Paris Morton Music", "Days In The East", "Club Paradise", 4PM in Calabasas", "Furthest Thing"—these underappreciated tracks showcase his raw lyricism and early Toronto vibes, offering a glimpse into his unpolished brilliance.

Latest Obsessions: "Nokia", "Gimme A Hug", "Crying In Chanel" (from Some Sexy Songs 4 U)—2025’s releases blend R&B swagger with club-ready beats, hinting at a new chapter of vulnerability and resilience post-beef.


Drama & Controversies


Drake’s career has been as dramatic as his music. Early tensions with Chris Brown escalated into a 2012 nightclub brawl, though they later reconciled with a comedic 2014 ESPY Awards skit. His 2016 feud with Meek Mill—sparked by ghostwriting allegations—produced diss tracks like "Back to Back", cementing Drake’s lyrical prowess. The 2018 clash with Pusha T exposed personal details, including his son Adonis, via "The Story of Adidon", prompting Drake to lean into fatherhood publicly. More recently, his 2024 beef with Kendrick Lamar—ignited by "Like That" and escalated with "Not Like Us"—saw Lamar’s Super Bowl halftime jab and Drake’s legal action against Universal Music Group (UMG) for allegedly boosting Lamar’s track, settled in February 2025. As of March 2025, Drake’s X posts criticize the industry’s “enslavement” of artists, reigniting his feud with Drake over alleged corporate agendas, while his Anita Max Wynn Tour in Australia faced fan boos, adding to his polarizing image.


Legacy


Drake’s legacy as a trailblazer is etched in hip-hop history. Evolving from a Degrassi actor to a Billboard-named Artist of the 2010s Decade, he’s sold 170 million records and amassed 75 billion streams, outpacing legends like 2Pac and Jay-Z in the streaming era. His innovation—merging R&B sensibilities with rap—has inspired a generation, from Post Malone to Rod Wave. Cultural milestones like the "In My Feelings" challenge and phrases like "6ix" have permeated global pop culture. Despite controversies, his ability to turn personal setbacks into art—seen in Some Sexy Songs 4 U’s pivot from the Lamar beef—ensures his enduring relevance. As he headlines the 2025 Wireless Festival and teases new projects, Drake’s legacy as a cultural shapeshifter remains secure, poised to evolve with each release.
