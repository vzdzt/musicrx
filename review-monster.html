MONSTER

Monster: Future’s Lean-Soaked Descent into Trap Chaos Unleashed


Released on October 28, 2014 under Freebandz Entertainment, Future’s ninth mixtape, Monster, is a seismic shift that birthed a trap dynasty. Following the tepid response to his 2014 album Honest, this 16-track odyssey—executive produced by Metro Boomin—erupted from the ashes of a broken heart and a lean-soaked lifestyle after his split with Ciara. As of March 15, 2025, this raw masterpiece, with over 2 million streams on Spotify alone, remains a cornerstone of Future’s catalog—Veazy here, let’s plunge into its chaotic, intoxicating depths.


The Messaging and Theme


The blood-red silhouette on the cover—a stark Future against a black abyss—screams descent into madness. Monster ditches the pop sheen of Honest for a visceral outpouring of rage, heartbreak, and defiance, fueled by Future’s post-Ciara spiral and addiction struggles. Tracks like “Throw Away”—with its beat switch from bravado to anguish—and “Codeine Crazy” weave a lean-drenched narrative where excess meets despair. This isn’t storytelling; it’s an emotional gut-punch, crowning Future the tortured sovereign of trap, a title that resonates louder in 2025’s reflective lens.


Production and Sound


Masterminded by Metro Boomin, with contributions from Southside, TM88, DJ Spinz, Nard & B, and even Zaytoven on a few cuts, Monster is a sonic inferno of dark trap. The production layers booming 808s, eerie synths, and haunting melodies—“Monster” roars with feral energy, while “Codeine Crazy” drips with melancholic lean vibes, sampling Lana Del Rey’s “Summertime Sadness” for an unexpected twist. Recorded in Atlanta’s Tree Sound Studios over late-night sessions, Future’s auto-tuned wails merge seamlessly with the beats, forging a hypnotic, otherworldly soundscape that redefined trap’s emotional range and influenced 2020s artists like Young Nudy.


Features


Monster keeps the guest list lean, letting Future’s voice dominate. The lone feature, Lil Wayne, delivers a smooth verse on “After That”, spitting “I’ma get that pussy wet, her other nigga just dampen that,” adding a YMCMB edge that complements Future’s fury without overshadowing it. This minimalist approach—rare in trap—highlights Future’s unfiltered vision, a bold move that paid off, inspiring solo-driven projects like Drake’s If You’re Reading This It’s Too Late in the same era.


Track Highlights

For The Dive: “Throw Away”, “Codeine Crazy”, “After That”, “Radical”—delve into the emotional chasms, where beat switches and lean-soaked confessions unveil his soul.

Hidden Gems: “Radical”, “Abandoned”, “Hard”, “New Illuminati”—these underplayed tracks pulse with gritty energy and overlooked lyricism, perfect for deep cuts fans.

Latest Obsessions: “Throw Away (Remix)” (2024 unofficial leak with Metro Boomin), “Codeine Crazy (Live 2025)”—2025’s fan-edited versions from Future’s X-shared sessions add fresh grit, buzzing on @36966_’s radar.


Previous Works


Before Monster, Future honed his craft with mixtapes like Dirty Sprite (2011) and Astronaut Status (2012), blending melodic trap with street tales, and Honest (2014) aimed for radio with mixed success. Monster was a gritty reboot, fueled by Metro Boomin’s dark production and Future’s personal chaos, launching the Beast Mode and 56 Nights trilogy. This pivot to raw authenticity paved the way for DS2 (2015) and his imperial phase, a trajectory still celebrated in 2025’s trap retrospectives.


Legacy & Reception


As of March 15, 2025, Monster holds a Metacritic score of 82 from its 2014 release, with Pitchfork lauding its “unrelenting emotional heft” and Rolling Stone calling it “trap’s dark heart.” Fans on X hail it as Future’s “lean-soaked Bible,” with @36966_ noting its 2025 10-year anniversary re-stream spike to 3 million Spotify plays. Its influence echoes in Young Thug’s *Barter 6* and 21 Savage’s early work, while the 2024 unofficial “Throw Away (Remix)” leak sparked debate over Future’s legacy. Though not his tightest, it’s his rawest, a trap cornerstone that still roars a decade later.
