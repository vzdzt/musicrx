TOP

Top: NBA YoungBoy's Relentless Reign Continues


On September 11, 2020, NBA YoungBoy dropped Top, a 21-track behemoth that arrived amid legal battles and unrelenting fan demand. Following a string of prolific releases, this album solidified his dominance in the trap scene with raw energy and emotional grit. Here's a deep dive into this relentless Southern triumph:


The Messaging and Theme


The album's cover—a brooding YoungBoy against a stormy sky—sets the tone: defiance, pain, and unyielding hustle. Top is NBA YoungBoy baring his soul, from street anthems like "House Arrest Tingz" to heartbreak confessions in "All In." Tracks like "Kacey Talk" flex his charisma, while "Dead Trollz" unleashes venom on his foes. It's less a narrative than a raw diary—YoungBoy's life of loyalty, betrayal, and survival in Baton Rouge, delivered with a middle finger to the world. This is his crown, worn with scars and all.


Production and Sound


Produced by a crew including TayTayMadeIt, Dmac, and Mike Laury, Top is a sonic assault rooted in Southern trap. The beats are lean and menacing—"Dead Trollz" thumps with distorted 808s, while "My Window" layers eerie keys over pounding drums. "Kacey Talk" flips a soulful sample into a mellow flex, and "The Last Backyard..." rides a hypnotic guitar riff. YoungBoy's voice—raspy, urgent, and melodic—cuts through the chaos, shifting from aggressive shouts to Auto-Tuned croons. It's a polished take on his signature sound, balancing grit with accessibility.


Features


Top keeps features sparse, letting YoungBoy's presence dominate. Snoop Dogg brings West Coast cool to "Callin," while Lil Wayne trades bars on "My Window," adding veteran swagger. These guests don't steal the show but complement YoungBoy's intensity, offering brief detours in his solo spotlight. The scarcity of features underscores his self-reliance—Top is his kingdom, and he rules it alone.


Tracks to Watch

  • "Kacey Talk" - A smooth, charismatic banger with a soulful edge.

  • "Dead Trollz" - A ferocious diss track dripping with raw aggression.

  • "All In" - A melodic cry of pain and perseverance.

  • "My Window" (feat. Lil Wayne) - A haunting standout with a killer Wayne verse.

Previous Works


NBA YoungBoy's rise began with mixtapes like 38 Baby (2016), showcasing his gritty, melodic trap style. Albums like AI YoungBoy (2017) and Until Death Call My Name (2018) built his cult following with relentless output. Top refines this formula, trading some of the lo-fi chaos of earlier work for sharper production and broader appeal. Where past releases were rawer and less structured, Top polishes his sound without losing its edge—YoungBoy leveling up while staying true to his roots.


Summary


Top is a 63-minute onslaught—21 tracks of unfiltered emotion and trap firepower. It's NBA YoungBoy at his peak, channeling pain into anthems and proving his staying power amid chaos. The album's length can drag, with some tracks blending together, but its highs—like "Kacey Talk"—hit hard. Top doesn't reinvent YoungBoy's wheel; it spins it faster, cementing him as a voice for the streets. It's a victory cry from a kid who's seen it all, still standing atop the game.


Ratings

Originality (16/20): A refined take on YoungBoy's trap sound, though it treads familiar ground.

Production Quality (18/20): Crisp and hard-hitting, with occasional repetition.

Lyrics and Messaging (17/20): Raw and real, though depth varies track to track.

Enjoyment Factor (18/20): A gripping listen for fans, if you can stomach the runtime.

Consistency (15/20): Strong front half, but filler creeps in later.

Overall Rating (84/100): 8.4/10

Top isn't a game-changer—it's a testament to NBA YoungBoy's relentless grind and magnetic pull. It's flawed, bloated, and unapologetic, much like the man himself. In a crowded trap landscape, this stands tall—a raw, roaring declaration that YoungBoy's reign isn't slowing down anytime soon.
