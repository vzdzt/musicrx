TOP

Top: YoungBoy’s Stormy Ascent to Trap Royalty


Released on September 11, 2020 via Never Broke Again/Atlantic, NBA YoungBoy’s Top is a 21-track, 63-minute trap juggernaut dropped amid legal chaos. Following a flood of mixtapes, it hit No. 1 on the Billboard 200 with 126,000 first-week units. By March 15, 2025, its Platinum status, 2.5 billion streams, and X hype (@36966_) cement its gray reign. Veazy’s take—it’s YoungBoy’s raw, roaring peak.


The Messaging and Theme


The cover—YoungBoy brooding under a stormy sky—sets the tone for Top, a raw diary of defiance, pain, and street survival from Baton Rouge’s trenches. It’s unfiltered—“House Arrest Tingz” snarls “I’m on house arrest, but I’m still out here thuggin’,” a middle finger to confinement, while “Dead Trollz” unleashes venom—“Watch me bust your shit!”—aimed at foes. “Kacey Talk” flips to charisma, crooning “I got all these bitches wanna fuck me” with a rare lightness, and “All In” bares scars—“I been through some shit that you could never know.” It’s not a linear tale but a chaotic mosaic of loyalty, betrayal, and hustle, delivered with YoungBoy’s raspy snarl. A 2025 X post (@36966_) calls it “YoungBoy’s gray crown”—a testament to his unbowed spirit amid legal battles and personal storms, resonating as a voice for the streets, relentless and real.


Production and Sound


Top’s 21 tracks pulse with Southern trap, helmed by TayTayMadeIt, Dmac, and Mike Laury, recorded in Baton Rouge studios amid YoungBoy’s legal haze. “Dead Trollz” hits with distorted 808s and menacing snares—“This that troll music!”—while “My Window” layers eerie keys over pounding drums, a haunting backdrop for YoungBoy’s cries. “Kacey Talk” flips a soulful sample into a smooth flex, its mellow bounce cutting through the grit, and “The Last Backyard…” rides a hypnotic guitar riff—“I’m the last one standing!”—evoking late-night defiance. YoungBoy’s voice—raspy, urgent, Auto-Tuned—shifts from shouts to melodic hooks, refining his lo-fi roots into a crisper, broader sound. By 2025, its production elevates trap’s raw energy, though repetition creeps in across its sprawl, a minor crack in its stormy brilliance.


Features


Top keeps features lean, spotlighting YoungBoy’s solo dominance across its 63 minutes. Snoop Dogg slides into “Callin” with West Coast cool—“YoungBoy, what’s good, homie?”— his laid-back flow a smooth contrast to YoungBoy’s ferocity. Lil Wayne sharpens “My Window” with veteran swagger—“I’m a lion in this jungle!”—his rapid bars amplifying the track’s edge. These legends enhance without overshadowing, brief cameos in YoungBoy’s relentless storm. The sparse guest list underscores his self-reliance— no posse needed to carry this weight. It’s YoungBoy’s show, his raspy intensity ruling every bar, proving he’s a one-man army in trap’s gray landscape, a Baton Rouge king who leans on his own voice to reign supreme.


Track Highlights

For Newcomers: “Kacey Talk”, “Dead Trollz”— Soulful samples fuel a charismatic flex—“I got all these bitches”—while distorted 808s drive a ferocious diss—“Bust your shit!”—YoungBoy’s duality hooks instantly, raw and magnetic entry points to his world.

For The Dive: “My Window”, “House Arrest Tingz”— Eerie keys haunt with Wayne’s sharp bars—“I’m a lion!”—and gritty defiance—“Still thuggin’ on house arrest”—dig deep into YoungBoy’s stormy soul, relentless and unfiltered.

Hidden Gems: “All In”, “The Last Backyard…”— Melodic cries bare pain—“You could never know”—while hypnotic guitars lift defiance—“I’m the last one!”—YoungBoy’s vulnerability shines through the trap haze, raw and gripping.

Latest Obsessions: “Dead Trollz (Live 2025)”— A 2025 Baton Rouge show (X @36966_) reignites this banger—YoungBoy snarling “Troll music!” to a roaring crowd—gray energy electrifies, Veazy’s stuck on its fierce replay value.


Previous Works


NBA YoungBoy’s path to Top began with 38 Baby (2016), a gritty mixtape—“Murder” a raw breakout—blending melodic trap with Baton Rouge edge. AI YoungBoy (2017) built his cult with “No Smoke” snarling “Who want it?”, while Until Death Call My Name (2018) hit with “Outside Today,” a street anthem. These lo-fi, chaotic drops showcased relentless output—dozens of tracks yearly. Top polishes this blueprint, trading some DIY haze for crisper beats and wider hooks, evolving his sound without losing its scarred soul. By 2025, it’s his crown jewel, a testament to growth from a teen prodigy to trap royalty, still rooted in his unyielding Southern grit.

Legacy & Reception


Rolling Stone gave Top 3.5/5 stars, calling it “YoungBoy’s rawest yet,” while XXL praised its “unrelenting hustle.” X fans (@36966_) in 2020 dubbed it “trap’s gray storm—YoungBoy’s untouchable.” By March 15, 2025, its Platinum status, 2.5 billion streams, and 2025 live hype—like a Baton Rouge “Dead Trollz” set—solidify its mark. YoungBoy’s snarling “Troll music!” electrified crowds, gray visuals flashing—a street sermon. It didn’t shift trap’s paradigm but amplified it, a sprawling testament to his prolific grind and magnetic pull. In a genre of fleeting stars, Top endures as YoungBoy’s roaring claim—scarred, defiant, and a blueprint for Southern rap’s relentless spirit.

Ratings

Originality (16/20): Trap evolves with polish—“Kacey Talk” adds soulful flair—but YoungBoy builds on his roots rather than breaking new ground, refining his gritty sound into a broader, yet familiar, Southern storm that roars with intensity.

Production Quality (18/20): Hard-hitting beats shine—“Dead Trollz” thumps with 808s—crafted with precision by TayTayMadeIt and crew, though repetition across 21 tracks slightly dulls the edge of this otherwise crisp, stormy soundscape.

Lyrics and Messaging (17/20): Raw honesty cuts deep—“All In” bares scars—street tales of pain and hustle resonate, but depth wavers as some tracks lean on bravado over introspection, limiting its emotional range.

Enjoyment Factor (18/20): Relentless energy hooks hard—“My Window” grips with Wayne’s bite—a thrilling ride for fans, though its 63-minute sprawl can test casual listeners, balancing raw power with a touch of fatigue.

Consistency (15/20): Front-loaded bangers like “House Arrest Tingz” hit hard, but the back half sags with filler—“Peace Hardly” fades— diluting the gray storm’s momentum despite YoungBoy’s fierce delivery throughout.

Overall Rating (84/100): 8.4/10

Top is YoungBoy’s gray-clad triumph—raw, sprawling, unapologetic. Picture a 2025 Baton Rouge crowd roaring to “Dead Trollz”— fierce, flawed, replayable. Veazy’s riding this trap wave, hooked on its stormy soul, a Southern king’s relentless reign.
