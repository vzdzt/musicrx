CALL ME IF YOU GET LOST

Tyler, The Creator - CMIYGL


On June 25, 2021, Tyler, The Creator unleashed Call Me If You Get Lost, a 16-track odyssey that landed two years after his introspective IGOR. Blending brash rap energy with lush production, this album cemented Tyler’s evolution from provocateur to polymath. Here’s a deep dive into this vibrant, jet-setting masterpiece:


The Messaging and Theme

The album’s cover—a faux ID for “Tyler Baudelaire”—and DJ Drama’s Gangsta Grillz narration frame it as a travelogue of excess and identity. Call Me If You Get Lost is Tyler flexing his wealth, love, and creative freedom, as seen in “Corso” and “Lumberjack.” Yet beneath the bravado lies vulnerability—“Wilshire” unravels a tale of unrequited love with raw honesty. It’s a passport to Tyler’s mind: globetrotting swagger meets emotional turbulence, all wrapped in a nostalgic nod to mixtape culture. This is Tyler reveling in his prime, lost in the best way possible.


Production and Sound

Entirely produced by Tyler, The Creator, CMIYGL is a sonic buffet of jazz, soul, and hard-hitting hip-hop. The beats are eclectic—“Lumberjack” chops a Gravediggaz sample into a menacing banger, while “Sweet / I Thought You Wanted to Dance” blends breezy horns with reggae-tinged romance. “Lemonhead” pops with triumphant brass, and “Wusyaname” glides on buttery R&B vibes. Tyler’s voice shifts from guttural shouts to smooth croons, layered over his meticulous soundscapes. It’s a bold pivot from IGOR’s synth-heavy haze, trading melancholy for a sun-soaked, high-octane vibe.


Features

Call Me If You Get Lost’s guest list is a curated crew, each amplifying Tyler’s vision. Lil Wayne snarls on “Hot Wind Blows,” while 42 Dugg brings Detroit grit to “Lemonhead.” YoungBoy Never Broke Again and Ty Dolla $ign shine on “Wusyaname,” and Lil Uzi Vert adds playful chaos to “Juggernaut.” Pharrell lends soulful cool to “Juggernaut,” and Brent Faiyaz anchors the tender “Sweet.” These voices don’t dominate but enhance Tyler’s world, with DJ Drama’s hypeman energy tying it all together.


Tracks to Watch

  • “Corso” - A swagger-soaked opener dripping with jazzy horns and Tyler’s brash bars, laying down a lavish welcome mat for the album’s jet-set vibe.

  • “Wusyaname” (feat. YoungBoy Never Broke Again & Ty Dolla $ign) - A silky-smooth flirtation that floats on lush R&B waves, with a hook so addictive it’s like musical honey.

  • “Lumberjack” - A ferocious, sample-driven beast where Tyler’s snarling flow slices through a Gravediggaz cut, flexing his rap roots with unrelenting grit.

  • “Sweet / I Thought You Wanted to Dance” (feat. Brent Faiyaz & Fana Hues) - A sprawling, 10-minute love letter that shifts from soulful sweetness to reggae-tinged longing, a cinematic standout that lingers.

Previous Works

Tyler’s journey began with Odd Future’s anarchic Bastard (2009), evolving through the shock-rap of Goblin (2011) to the melodic bloom of Flower Boy (2017). IGOR (2019) marked his artistic peak with its breakup narrative and Grammy win. Call Me If You Get Lost fuses Flower Boy’s polish with Goblin’s rap roots, adding a mixtape swagger. Where IGOR mourned love lost, CMIYGL celebrates life gained—less a reinvention and more a victory lap for Tyler’s boundless creativity.


Summary

Call Me If You Get Lost is a 52-minute joyride—16 tracks of swagger, soul, and storytelling. It’s Tyler at his most confident, blending rap’s grit with pop’s sheen, all while flexing his production prowess. The album’s pacing is near-perfect, though its dense guest list occasionally overshadows its star. CMIYGL doesn’t chase trends—it crafts them, offering a passport to Tyler’s universe of luxury and longing. It’s a love letter to hip-hop’s past and a blueprint for its future, proving Tyler’s lost only in his own brilliance.


Ratings

Originality (19/20): A masterful mixtape twist that fuses Tyler’s eclectic past with a fresh rap swagger, carving a lane that’s both nostalgic and forward-thinking.

Production Quality (20/20): A flawless tapestry of jazz, soul, and hip-hop—every beat sparkles with detail, proving Tyler’s ear is unmatched in its vibrancy and precision.

Lyrics and Messaging (18/20): Sharp-witted and heartfelt, balancing brash flexes with tender confessions, though the bravado occasionally overshadows deeper introspection.

Enjoyment Factor (19/20): A euphoric, replayable blast that hooks you from the jump—its richness rewards every listen, with only a slight guest overload as a hiccup.

Consistency (18/20): A tight, cohesive journey with a front-loaded punch—the back half dips slightly but never derails the album’s sunlit momentum.

Overall Rating (94/100): 9.4/10

Call Me If You Get Lost is Tyler, The Creator firing on all cylinders—a kaleidoscope of sound and emotion that’s as bold as it is beautiful. It’s not just an album; it’s a flex, a vibe, a destination. In a career of reinvention, this stands as his most assured work yet, a call worth answering again and again.
