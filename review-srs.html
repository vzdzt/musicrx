Some Rap Songs

Some Rap Songs: Earl Sweatshirt’s Hazy Grief Unspooled


On November 30, 2018, Earl Sweatshirt dropped Some Rap Songs, his third studio effort under Tan Cressida and Columbia Records—a 15-track, 24-minute lo-fi requiem largely crafted by Earl, with cuts from Navy Blue, Adé Hakim, and Standing on the Corner. Born from the ashes of his father’s death, it hit No. 17 on the Billboard 200 and snagged Gold status by 2022. As of March 11, 2025, its raw intimacy still echoes—a jagged gem worth dissecting.


The Messaging and Theme


The blurry, toothy selfie cover reflects Some Rap Songs’s unpolished soul: a meditation on grief, isolation, and defiance. After losing Keorapetse Kgositsile, Earl spills anguish into “Shattered Dreams”—“I spent my whole life depressed”—and “Peanut”—“This is not a phase.” “The Mint” wrestles mortality with “Only thing on my mind was death,” while “Playing Possum” layers his parents’ voices as a tender requiem. It’s a cracked window into Earl’s mind—unsparing and real.


Production and Sound


Earl Sweatshirt, alongside Navy Blue, Adé Hakim, and Standing on the Corner, sculpts Some Rap Songs’s lo-fi tapestry—jazz fragments, warped loops, and vinyl hiss weaving a dense fog. “Nowhere2go” lurches with glitchy unease, “Riot!” lifts Hugh Masekela’s trumpet into a bittersweet haze, and “Azucar” simmers with soulful distortion. At 24 minutes, it’s a fleeting storm—compact yet heavy with intent.


Features


Some Rap Songs leans light on guests, with Navy Blue, Standing on the Corner, and sampled voices of Cheryl Harris and Keorapetse Kgositsile. Navy Blue’s somber drawl haunts “The Mint,” while Standing on the Corner’s avant-jazz lifts “Ontheway!” Parental echoes on “Playing Possum” ground it in intimacy, keeping Earl’s lens unclouded.


Tracks to Watch

  • “Shattered Dreams” - Murky loops cradle Earl’s weary croak, a lo-fi lament that bleeds quiet despair.

  • “The Mint” - Jittery beats stab through a smoky veil, Earl’s grim resolve cutting sharp and slow.

  • “Azucar” - Soul hums beneath a warped haze, Earl’s dense rhymes drifting like ash in the wind.

  • “Riot!” - Trumpets wail over crackling static, a fleeting tribute glowing with familial ghosts.

Previous Works


Earl’s path to Some Rap Songs started with the brash Earl mixtape (2010), grew through Doris (2013), and sank into I Don’t Like Shit, I Don’t Go Outside (2015). After a three-year silence shaped by loss, Some Rap Songs spun his sound into lo-fi abstraction, setting the stage for Feet of Clay (2019).


Summary


Some Rap Songs is a 24-minute unraveling of Earl’s soul—lo-fi shards piercing through grief’s fog. Its raw brevity captivates, though its density demands patience. Hitting No. 17 on the Billboard 200, earning Gold by 2022, and boasting a Metacritic 86 and Pitchfork 8.8, it’s a cult cornerstone as of March 11, 2025. A must for seekers of truth in the haze.


Ratings

Originality (19/20): Earl twists rap into a lo-fi dirge—“Shattered Dreams” redefines raw—bold yet rooted.


Production Quality (18/20): Hiss and warp craft a fractured beauty—“Azucar” glows—but chaos teeters.


Lyrics and Messaging (19/20): Words bleed heavy—“The Mint”’s dread bites—dense and unflinching.


Enjoyment Factor (17/20): Depth pulls you in—“Riot!” haunts—but its weight tests the casual ear.


Consistency (18/20): Tracks thread a tight lament—brief yet whole, with faint stumbles.


Overall Rating (91/100): 9.1/10

Some Rap Songs is Earl’s lo-fi wound laid bare—a compact, gritty elegy of survival. It’s heavy, unpolished, and real, a quiet storm for those who feel the cracks. Play it soft, let the static hum, and drift into a haze where pain turns poetic.
