A.L.L.A

At Long Last A$AP: Rocky's Psychedelic Ascension


On May 26, 2015, A$AP Rocky surprise-dropped At Long Last A$AP, an 18-track sophomore album that arrived amid hype and tragedy following A$AP Yams' death. Building on his debut Long. Live. A$AP, this project plunged into a hazy, experimental realm, cementing Rocky's flair for style and sound. Here's a deep dive into this trippy, ambitious odyssey:

The Messaging and Theme

The album's cover—a purple-tinted Rocky with Yams' birthmark—sets a tone of tribute and transcendence. A.L.L.A. is a journey through fame's highs and lows, steeped in psychedelic swagger. Tracks like "L$D" and "Electric Body" revel in drugged-out hedonism, while "Jukebox Joints" and "Everyday" blend nostalgia with introspection. Dedicated to Yams, it's Rocky grappling with loss, success, and identity—less a cohesive story than a vibe-driven mosaic. It's Harlem's pretty boy proving he's more than a fashion icon, chasing a higher plane.

Production and Sound

Co-executive produced by Danger Mouse and A$AP Yams, with heavy input from Hector Delgado and Juicy J, A.L.L.A. is a sonic kaleidoscope. The beats fuse trap's thump with psych-rock haze—"L$D" drips with woozy guitars, while "Holy Ghost" layers gospel soul over eerie synths. "Canal St." chops a Bone Thugs sample into a gritty banger, and "Fine Whine" floats on dreamy keys. Rocky's flow—smooth yet commanding—rides these waves effortlessly, shifting from brash bars to melodic hums. It's a bold leap from Long. Live's polished trap, embracing a weirder, more adventurous soundscape.

Features

Rocky assembles an eclectic guest list that amplifies the album's boundary-pushing ethos. Kanye West brings soulful swagger to "Jukebox Joints," while ScHoolboy Q delivers menacing verses on "Electric Body." The Rod Stewart, Miguel, and Mark Ronson collaboration on "Everyday" creates an unlikely earworm, and Lil Wayne drops typically eccentric bars on "M'$." Underground icons like UGK and Mos Def add gravitas, while Joe Fox—a street musician Rocky discovered in London—appears throughout, lending raw, bluesy textures. It's a lineup that defies conventional hip-hop featuring strategies, mirroring Rocky's genre-blending vision.

Legacy and Impact

A.L.L.A. marked Rocky's artistic evolution from trendy hitmaker to experimental auteur. While some fans longed for the accessible bangers of his debut, others embraced his psychedelic direction. The album's influence can be seen in hip-hop's increasing comfort with alternative sounds and drug-influenced aesthetics. Its trippy visuals—particularly the "L$D" video—set new standards for rap's visual language. In Rocky's catalog, it stands as his most personal statement: a tribute to his late mentor, an exploration of his expanding consciousness, and proof that commercial rap could still take creative risks. Six years later, its hazy, genre-fluid approach feels ahead of its time.

Overall Rating: 8.7/10

At Long Last A$AP is Rocky at his most experimental and authentic. While occasionally disjointed, its psychedelic ambition and sonic daring makes it one of the decade's most intriguing hip-hop releases. Less concerned with chart success than creative exploration, it's the sound of an artist following his muse into uncharted territory—and bringing his listeners along for the trip.

Tracks to Watch

  • "L$D" - A dreamy, guitar-driven ode to psychedelics that showcases Rocky's melodic side.
  • "Excuse Me" - Smooth, confident flows over a hypnotic beat with one of the album's catchiest hooks.
  • "Canal St." - A gritty street anthem that flips Bones' "Dirt" into something wholly original.
  • "Everyday" - An unlikely collaboration that somehow melds Rod Stewart with trap drums to perfection.
