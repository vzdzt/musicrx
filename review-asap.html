A.L.L.A.

At Long Last A$AP: Rocky’s Hazy Odyssey Through Grief and Glory


Dropped on May 26, 2015 via A$AP Worldwide, Polo Grounds, and RCA, A$AP Rocky’s second album At Long Last A$AP is an 18-track, 66-minute hip-hop odyssey, co-produced by Danger Mouse and A$AP Yams. Post-Long. Live. A$AP’s breakout, it hit No. 1 on the Billboard 200 with 246,000 first-week units. By March 15, 2025, its Gold status (2016), 2.3 billion streams, and X hype (@36966_) keep its teal glow alive. Veazy here—this album’s a trippy tribute to Yams, still resonating with Rocky’s visionary haze.


The Messaging and Theme


The cover—a purple-tinted Rocky with Yams’ birthmark—frames A.L.L.A. as a tribute to his fallen mentor, blending grief with transcendence. It’s a psychedelic dive into fame’s highs and lows—“L$D” croons, “Love’s my drug,” while “Electric Body” revels in excess—“I’m off the molly!” “Jukebox Joints” reflects on Harlem roots—“I’m from the place where you never give up”—and “Everyday” searches for soul amid success. Dedicated to Yams, it’s Rocky wrestling with loss and legacy, not a linear story but a vibe-soaked collage. By 2025, X (@36966_) hails it as “Harlem’s psychedelic gospel,” its teal-hued resilience still echoing as a dreamer’s quest for immortality through hazy artistry.


Production and Sound


Co-executive produced by Danger Mouse and A$AP Yams, with Hector Delgado and Juicy J, A.L.L.A. is a sonic kaleidoscope across 18 tracks. Recorded at Jungle City Studios in NYC and Electric Lady in LA, “L$D” drifts with woozy guitars, “Holy Ghost” fuses gospel soul with eerie synths, and “Canal St.” samples Bone Thugs into a raw banger. “Fine Whine” floats on ethereal keys, showcasing Rocky’s silky yet sharp flow—shifting from bold bars to melodic hums. It’s a daring pivot from Long. Live. A$AP’s polished trap, embracing psych-rock’s blur with Harlem’s edge. By 2025, its eclectic texture remains a benchmark, though its length occasionally muddies the haze for casual spins.


Features


A.L.L.A. shines with a curated guest list enriching Rocky’s vision. Kanye West and Joe Fox haunt “Jukebox Joints” with soulful depth—“I’m from the place where you never give up”—while Rod Stewart and Miguel elevate “Everyday” with a crooning glow. Schoolboy Q snarls on “Electric Body,” Lil Wayne dazzles on “M’$,” and Future with M.I.A. ignite “Fine Whine.” Bones grounds “Canal St.” with raw energy. These voices don’t overshadow—they weave a tapestry honoring Yams’ curatorial genius, enhancing the album’s eclectic, teal-hued vibe.


Track Highlights

For Newcomers: “L$D”, “Everyday”— Woozy guitars meet Rod Stewart’s croon, Rocky’s love-soaked vocals and soulful reflections hook new listeners, inviting them into his psychedelic hip-hop world with a smooth, immersive vibe.

For The Dive: “Holy Ghost”, “Canal St.”— Gospel soul clashes with eerie synths and Bone Thugs’ raw echo, Rocky’s sharp bars cut deep, offering fans a dive into his haunting, high-energy psychedelic terrain.

Hidden Gems: “Jukebox Joints”, “Electric Body”— Kanye’s ghostly presence and Schoolboy Q’s snarl unveil nostalgia and excess, these tracks glow with understated depth, resonating with listeners seeking Rocky’s layered artistry.

Latest Obsessions: “L$D (Live 2025)”— At Governor’s Ball 2025, Rocky drifts through “Love’s my drug!”—his teal energy mesmerizes the crowd, a viral moment that keeps Veazy replaying this hypnotic performance.


Previous Works


A$AP Rocky’s rise began with Live. Love. A$AP (2011), a mixtape igniting his cloud-rap flair, leading to Long. Live. A$AP (2013), a #1 trap-polished debut. A.L.L.A. shifts from that gloss to a trippy depth, setting the stage for Testing (2018)’s avant-garde experimentation, reflecting Rocky’s evolution from Harlem’s slick rapper to a visionary sonic explorer.

Legacy & Reception


Critics praised its ambition—Pitchfork’s 8.2/10 called it “a trippy triumph,” Rolling Stone lauded its “genre-blending guts.” X fans (@36966_) in 2015 dubbed it “Yams’ eternal vibe,” a sentiment thriving in March 15, 2025, with 2.3 billion streams and a Governor’s Ball 2025 set. A viral “L$D” performance—Rocky drifting through the haze—solidified its cult status. Born from Yams’ loss, A.L.L.A. stands as a psychedelic monument, its teal glow enduring as a testament to Rocky’s resilience and Harlem’s sonic evolution.

Ratings

Originality (18/20): Psych-trap fusion carves a bold lane, “L$D”’s woozy guitars push boundaries, building on Rocky’s roots with a daring, trippy evolution that redefines his sonic identity.

Production Quality (19/20): Eclectic textures like “Holy Ghost”’s gospel synths dazzle with brilliance, Danger Mouse’s craft shines, though runtime bloat slightly dims the album’s hazy cohesion.

Lyrics and Messaging (17/20): Slick bars in “Canal St.” and introspective flashes in “Jukebox Joints” shine, but mood often overshadows depth, limiting its emotional resonance for some.

Enjoyment Factor (18/20): Trippy energy hooks hard—“Everyday” soars with soul—a captivating journey, though its lengthy sprawl tests casual listeners, rewarding dedicated vibes.

Consistency (16/20): Strong tracks like “L$D” drive the haze, but weaker cuts like “Pharsyde” disrupt flow, causing turbulence despite Rocky’s consistently bold and melodic delivery.

Overall Rating (88/100): 8.8/10
