A.L.L.A.

At Long Last A$AP: Rocky’s Hazy Odyssey Through Grief and Glory


On May 26, 2015, A$AP Rocky surprise-dropped At Long Last A$AP, an 18-track sophomore album via A$AP Worldwide, Polo Grounds, and RCA, arriving amid hype and heartbreak after A$AP Yams’ death. Following his debut Long. Live. A$AP, this project hit #1 on the Billboard 200, went Gold by 2016, and carved a hazy, experimental lane. As of March 16, 2025, it remains Rocky’s trippy tribute to resilience—let’s unravel its psychedelic layers.


The Messaging and Theme

The cover—a purple-tinted Rocky with Yams’ birthmark—sets a tone of tribute and transcendence. A.L.L.A. navigates fame’s highs and lows, steeped in psychedelic swagger. “L$D” and “Electric Body” plunge into drug-fueled excess, while “Jukebox Joints” and “Everyday” blend nostalgia with soul-searching. Dedicated to Yams, it’s Rocky grappling with loss, success, and self—a vibe-soaked collage rather than a linear tale. Harlem’s slickest dreamer proves he’s more than style, chasing immortality through a hazy lens.


Production and Sound

Co-executive produced by Danger Mouse and A$AP Yams, with Hector Delgado and Juicy J, A.L.L.A. is a sonic kaleidoscope. Trap’s edge meets psych-rock’s blur—“L$D” drifts with woozy guitars, while “Holy Ghost” merges gospel soul with eerie synths. “Canal St.” slices Bone Thugs into a raw banger, and “Fine Whine” floats on ethereal keys. Rocky’s flow—silky yet sharp—glides across these beats, shifting from bold bars to melodic hums, a daring pivot from Long. Live. A$AP’s sleek trap into uncharted sonic territory.


Features

A.L.L.A. boasts a stellar lineup, each guest amplifying Rocky’s vision. Kanye West and Joe Fox haunt “Jukebox Joints,” while Rod Stewart and Miguel lend soul to “Everyday.” Schoolboy Q snarls on “Electric Body,” Lil Wayne shines on “M’$,” and Future and M.I.A. ignite “Fine Whine.” Bones grounds “Canal St.” These voices don’t overshadow—they enrich the album’s eclectic texture, honoring Yams’ curatorial genius.


Tracks to Watch

  • “L$D” - Woozy guitars paint this psychedelic drift, Rocky’s love-soaked vocals crafting a fever dream.

  • “Jukebox Joints” (feat. Kanye West & Joe Fox) - Soulful layers simmer in nostalgia, Kanye’s ghostly presence adding depth.

  • “Everyday” (feat. Rod Stewart, Miguel & Mark Ronson) - Stewart’s croon elevates this soulful reflection, fame’s weight resonating deeply.

  • “Canal St.” (feat. Bones) - Bone Thugs’ echo drives this raw banger, Rocky’s sharp bars cutting through.

Previous Works

Rocky’s 2011 mixtape Live. Love. A$AP sparked his syrupy flow and cloud-rap rise, paving the way for his 2013 debut Long. Live. A$AP, a #1 trap-polished breakout. A.L.L.A. evolves from that foundation, trading Long. Live’s hit-driven gloss for a trippier, more daring plunge. It prioritizes legacy over pop appeal, setting the stage for Testing’s (2018) avant-garde sprawl.


Summary

At Long Last A$AP unfolds as a 66-minute voyage—18 tracks of fearless ambition and hazy soul. Rocky channels Yams’ spirit, fusing trap’s bite with psychedelic sheen in a tribute to his fallen mentor. Its sprawl falters—“Pharsyde” drags—but its peaks, like “L$D,” soar. As of March 16, 2025, its Gold status (2016) and cult reverence cement it as Rocky’s boldest triumph—a refusal to conform, forged in loss and lifted by legend.


Ratings

Originality (18/20): Psych-trap fusion carves a daring path, building on Rocky’s roots with bold experimentation.

Production Quality (19/20): Textures dazzle with eclectic brilliance, though minor bloat clouds the haze.

Lyrics and Messaging (17/20): Slick bars and introspective flashes shine, with mood often taking precedence over depth.

Enjoyment Factor (18/20): Trippy energy captivates throughout, its runtime a marathon that rewards the journey.

Consistency (16/20): Strong currents drive the vibe, with weaker tracks creating slight turbulence.

Overall Rating (88/100): 8.8/10

At Long Last A$AP is Rocky’s untamed spirit—sprawling, luminous, and unapologetic. A tribute to Yams, a defiance of norms, and a psychedelic crown, it grows bolder with time. Drop the needle, let the haze roll, and vibe with Harlem’s visionary dreamer.
