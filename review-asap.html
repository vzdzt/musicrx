A.L.L.A.

At Long Last A$AP: Rocky's Psychedelic Ascension


On May 26, 2015, A$AP Rocky surprise-dropped At Long Last A$AP, an 18-track sophomore album that arrived amid hype and tragedy following A$AP Yams' death. Building on his debut Long. Live. A$AP, this project plunged into a hazy, experimental realm, cementing Rocky's flair for style and sound. Here's a deep dive into this trippy, ambitious odyssey:


The Messaging and Theme

The album's cover—a purple-tinted Rocky with Yams' birthmark—sets a tone of tribute and transcendence. A.L.L.A. is a journey through fame's highs and lows, steeped in psychedelic swagger. Tracks like "L$D" and "Electric Body" revel in drugged-out hedonism, while "Jukebox Joints" and "Everyday" blend nostalgia with introspection. Dedicated to Yams, it's Rocky grappling with loss, success, and identity—less a cohesive story than a vibe-driven mosaic. It's Harlem's pretty boy proving he's more than a fashion icon, chasing a higher plane.


Production and Sound

Co-executive produced by Danger Mouse and A$AP Yams, with heavy input from Hector Delgado and Juicy J, A.L.L.A. is a sonic kaleidoscope. The beats fuse trap's thump with psych-rock haze—"L$D" drips with woozy guitars, while "Holy Ghost" layers gospel soul over eerie synths. "Canal St." chops a Bone Thugs sample into a gritty banger, and "Fine Whine" floats on dreamy keys. Rocky's flow—smooth yet commanding—rides these waves effortlessly, shifting from brash bars to melodic hums. It's a bold leap from Long. Live's polished trap, embracing a weirder, more adventurous soundscape.


Features

A.L.L.A. boasts a star-studded lineup, each guest amplifying Rocky's vision. Kanye West and Joe Fox haunt "Jukebox Joints," while Rod Stewart and Miguel lend soul to "Everyday." Schoolboy Q brings menace to "Electric Body," and Lil Wayne flexes on "M'$." Future and M.I.A. elevate "Fine Whine," and Bones anchors "Canal St." These voices don't outshine Rocky but deepen the album's eclectic texture, a tribute to Yams' curatorial spirit.


Tracks to Watch

  • "L$D" - A psychedelic love trip with a hypnotic groove and dreamy visuals.
  • "Jukebox Joints" (feat. Kanye West & Joe Fox) - A soulful standout with warm samples and reflective bars.
  • "Everyday" (feat. Rod Stewart, Miguel & Mark Ronson) - A soaring anthem of fame's grind.
  • "Canal St." (feat. Bones) - A gritty, sample-driven banger with Harlem swagger.

Previous Works

Rocky's 2011 mixtape Live. Love. A$AP introduced his syrupy flow and cloud-rap vibes, paving the way for his 2013 debut, Long. Live. A$AP. That album blended Southern trap with East Coast flair, cementing his mainstream rise. At Long Last A$AP builds on this foundation, swapping Long. Live's radio-ready sheen for a riskier, more experimental edge. Where his debut played it safe with hits, A.L.L.A. dives into uncharted waters—less about chart dominance and more about artistic legacy.


Summary

At Long Last A$AP is a 66-minute rollercoaster—18 tracks of bold ambition and hazy vibes. It's Rocky channeling Yams' spirit, blending trap's grit with psychedelic soul in a tribute to his mentor. The album's sprawl can feel unfocused, but its highs—like "L$D"—are undeniable. A.L.L.A. doesn't chase trends; it carves its own lane, a testament to Rocky's refusal to be boxed in. It's a victory lap for A$AP Mob's golden child, proving he can shine even in the shadow of loss.


Ratings

Originality (18/20): A daring mix of trap and psych-rock, though it leans on familiar influences.

Production Quality (19/20): Lush and inventive, with slight overindulgence in spots.

Lyrics and Messaging (17/20): Stylish and evocative, but depth takes a backseat to vibe.

Enjoyment Factor (18/20): A thrilling ride that rewards immersion, if you can handle the length.

Consistency (16/20): Mostly cohesive, but weaker cuts dilute the impact.

Overall Rating (88/100): 8.8/10

At Long Last A$AP isn't flawless—it's too long, too loose—but it's a dazzling showcase of Rocky's vision. It's a love letter to Yams, a middle finger to convention, and a bold step forward. In a career defined by cool, this remains his most audacious flex—a psychedelic gem that shines brighter with every spin.
