SOS

SZA - SOS


On December 9, 2022, SZA dropped SOS, a sprawling 23-track sophomore album that arrived five years after her groundbreaking debut, Ctrl. With sky-high anticipation, this project showcased SZA's evolution, blending raw vulnerability with bold swagger. Here's a deep dive into this soul-baring masterpiece:


The Messaging and Theme


The album's title and cover—SZA perched on a diving board above a vast ocean—signal a cry for help and a leap of faith. SOS is a diary of contradictions: love's wreckage in "Gone Girl", self-assured defiance in "F2F," and vengeance in "Kill Bill." It's SZA wrestling with heartbreak, insecurity, and empowerment, often within the same breath. Tracks like "Snooze" and "Nobody Gets Me" ache with longing, while "Smoking on My Ex Pack" spits fire. There's no tidy resolution—just a messy, honest portrait of a woman navigating her 30s, unafraid to bare her scars.


Production and Sound


SOS is a sonic tapestry, helmed by producers like Babyface, ThankGod4Cody, and Carter Lang. It spans R&B's sultry roots, pop-punk edge, and hip-hop grit—think the shimmering guitars of "F2F," the woozy synths of "Snooze," and the trap thump of "Low." SZA's voice—velvety yet piercing—shifts from soft croons to biting snarls, as heard in "Kill Bill"'s eerie whistle. The production is lush yet dynamic, balancing Ctrl's lo-fi intimacy with a broader, more adventurous palette. It's a genre-blending journey that mirrors her emotional chaos.


Features


SOS keeps features sparse but impactful, each guest amplifying SZA's vision. Travis Scott adds a hazy swagger to "Low" and "Open Arms," while Phoebe Bridgers brings indie melancholy to "Ghost in the Machine." Don Toliver glides over "Used," and the late Ol' Dirty Bastard haunts "Forgiveless" with a sampled verse. These collaborations don't overshadow SZA but enrich her narrative, offering glimpses of connection in her solitary storm.


Tracks to Watch

  • "Kill Bill" - A chilling revenge fantasy with a catchy hook and sinister charm.

  • "Snooze" - A dreamy love ballad that showcases SZA's vocal finesse.

  • "F2F" - A pop-punk detour that channels raw heartbreak into rebellion.

  • "Nobody Gets Me" - A stripped-down confessional that cuts to the bone.

Previous Works


SZA's 2017 debut, Ctrl, redefined R&B with its candid tales of love and self-doubt, earning critical acclaim and a cult following. Earlier projects like Z (2014) hinted at her potential with airy, eclectic vibes. SOS builds on Ctrl's foundation, amplifying its intimacy with bolder production and a wider emotional scope. Where Ctrl was a tight, 14-track therapy session, SOS sprawls into uncharted territory—less about youth's uncertainty and more about navigating life's wreckage with newfound confidence.


Summary


SOS is a 77-minute tidal wave—23 tracks that ebb between despair and defiance. It's SZA at her most unfiltered, weaving heartbreak, rage, and resilience into a sprawling yet cohesive narrative. The album's length tests patience, but its highs—like the viral "Kill Bill"—make it worth the dive. SOS doesn't chase trends; it sets them, cementing SZA as R&B's rawest storyteller. It's a distress signal and a victory lap, proving she can weather any storm and come out stronger.


Ratings

Originality (18/20): A daring blend of R&B, pop-punk, and trap, though it echoes Ctrl's DNA.

Production Quality (19/20): Rich and varied, with minor bloat in the tracklist.

Lyrics and Messaging (19/20): SZA's pen is sharp and soul-baring, with rare moments of repetition.

Enjoyment Factor (18/20): A rollercoaster of feels that rewards focus, if you can handle the runtime.

Consistency (16/20): Mostly tight, but the back half dips with filler.

Overall Rating (90/100): 9.0/10

SOS isn't perfect—it's too long, too chaotic—but that's its strength. SZA turns her turmoil into art, delivering an album that's as messy and beautiful as life itself. In a genre often polished to a sheen, this stands out—a bold, bruising testament to her voice, her vision, and her refusal to drown.
